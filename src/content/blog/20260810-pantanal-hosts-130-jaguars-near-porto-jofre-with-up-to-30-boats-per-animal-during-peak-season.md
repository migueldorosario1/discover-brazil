The Mato Grosso Pantanal was home to 130 jaguars habituated to human presence along the banks of the Cuiabá River in 2023 — a growth of more than 400% compared to the 29 individuals recorded in 2013. Researchers attribute this increase directly to observation-based ecotourism. The figures come from monitoring conducted at Parque Estadual Encontro das Águas, in Porto Jofre, which holds the largest jaguar population in the world. During the peak months of July and August, however, up to 30 boats can compete for sightings of the same animal simultaneously, according to a Mongabay report published in September 2025.

The experience of visiting the Pantanal changes considerably depending on the season and the region chosen. During the dry season, from July to October, animals concentrate around water sources and visibility improves significantly. During the wet season, from December to March, roughly 80% of the territory floods, roads become impassable, and wildlife disperses across the landscape. This period is not recommended for travelers seeking large mammals.

## Main activities and traveler profiles

Open-vehicle photographic safaris operate at dawn and dusk on most farms in both the Northern and Southern Pantanal. Specialist guides identify species that an untrained eye would easily miss, and a 10x42 binocular significantly expands what visitors can spot. Nighttime spotlighting — using torches to catch the eye-shine of animals — reveals ocelots, giant anteaters, and owls on virtually every property.

Horseback riding in the Pantanal uses horses adapted to flooded fields, capable of crossing shallow waterways without hesitation. It is the quietest method of approaching wildlife and offers insight into the extensive cattle ranching culture that shaped the Pantanal long before tourism arrived. For families, Fazenda San Francisco and Pousada Aguapé, both near Miranda in Mato Grosso do Sul, have an established track record with children's groups and offer a "pantaneiro day" that includes lasso practice and cattle herding.

Couples visiting the Southern Pantanal, in the Miranda and Aquidauana region, will find options such as Pantanal Jungle Lodge in Passo do Lontra, which operates on stilts over the water and received the TripAdvisor Club Trophy in 2025. Sunset boat excursions are included at most farms with river access and are consistently cited by guests as the most memorable moment of the trip.

## Birdwatching and jaguar observation

The biome has 650 catalogued bird species, including the hyacinth macaw, jabiru stork, roseate spoonbill, and endemics such as the Chaco puffbird. The Southern Pantanal, together with the Serra da Bodoquena, accounts for 32% of all bird species recorded in Brazil. In Miranda, the Projeto Arara Azul runs a conservation program and accepts visitors by prior arrangement.

For nature photographers, the most efficient combination pairs the jaguar safari at Porto Jofre with the 147-kilometer stretch of the Transpantaneira highway (MT-060), which functions as a free motorized safari route crossing 125 wooden bridges. The Caiman Ecological Refuge in Miranda runs the Onçafari Project and offers a consistent alternative for jaguar sightings with considerably less competition from other boats. Reservations for July through September require two to three months of advance notice.

## Costs and logistics

Budget-conscious travelers will find the lowest documented rates at SESC Porto Cercado in Poconé, Mato Grosso. In April 2023, three nights of full board for a couple cost R$1,869, a price that includes boat excursions and horseback riding — well below the R$9,000 charged by private lodges during the same period. Excursions at SESC Porto Cercado are scheduled in person upon arrival and cannot be booked online.

The Pantanal has no shared transfer services. Car rental starts at R$120 to R$150 per day. By bus, the Campo Grande–Miranda route costs approximately R$100 per person with Viação Andorinha. No farm package includes transportation to the property.

## Rules and equipment

- **Yellow fever vaccination** is mandatory. It should be administered at least 10 days before travel (confirm requirements at saude.gov.br).
- **Insect repellent with 50% DEET or higher** is essential. Neutral-colored clothing — beige and khaki — reduces disturbance to wildlife. Avoid white and black.
- **ICMBio-accredited boats** must maintain a minimum distance of 500 meters from jaguars. For other large mammals, the mandatory distance is 50 meters.
- **Sport fishing** requires an Environmental Authorization issued by IMASUL (pescaamadora.imasul.ms.gov.br). The closed fishing season in Mato Grosso do Sul runs from November to February. Fishing outside this period is an environmental offense carrying fines from R$700 to R$100,000.
