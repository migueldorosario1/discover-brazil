Brazil's Ministry of Tourism released a survey this week identifying 11 colonial municipalities that concentrate listed heritage sites, museum circuits, and railway routes across nine Brazilian states. The destinations are part of the national Tourism Map and, according to the ministry, function as creative economy hubs by driving demand for accommodation, gastronomy, and guided services.

Six of the listed cities hold UNESCO World Heritage status and record the highest visitation rates. Data from the municipal government of Ouro Preto, in Minas Gerais, indicate 1.3 million tourists per year visiting the city's baroque churches and museums in the historic center.

- **Ouro Preto (MG)** – baroque heritage collection and the Museu da Inconfidência
- **Olinda (PE)** – preserved urban layout and artists' studios
- **Paraty (RJ)** – 17th-century coastal colonial architecture
- **São Luís (MA)** – 18th-century Portuguese azulejo tilework
- **Salvador (BA)** – the Pelourinho district and the country's earliest churches
- **Goiás (GO)** – the Procissão do Fogaréu and the home of poet Cora Coralina

Municipal secretariats in the remaining five cities reported average hotel occupancy of 80% during extended public holidays, peaking during religious festivals and Carnival. Visitor stays range from two to four nights, according to local reports.

The organizers of the Festa Literária Internacional de Paraty (FLIP) calculated that the 2023 edition drew 27,000 attendees and injected R$35 million into the local economy. In São Luís, the June festival cycle centered on Bumba Meu Boi mobilized 14 folkloric groups recognized by Brazil's cultural heritage institute Iphan and attracted an estimated audience of 120,000 visitors.

A report by Belotur, the tourism authority for Belo Horizonte, notes that the Vale Steam Train — a 13-kilometer route between São João del-Rei and Tiradentes in Minas Gerais — carried 195,000 passengers in 2023. The Amazonas Theatre Foundation recorded 271 public performances in the same year, with an average occupancy rate of 87% in the main hall.

Recife's Secretariat of Culture estimates an investment of R$18 million — combining municipal funds and sponsorship from the Porto Digital technology hub — to restore facades in the Recife Antigo historic district by 2026. In Alcântara, Maranhão, the state government plans to restore 12 colonial townhouses using R$9.4 million from the National Culture Fund.
