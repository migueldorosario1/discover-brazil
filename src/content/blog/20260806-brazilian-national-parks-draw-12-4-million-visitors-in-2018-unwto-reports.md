Brazilian national parks received 12.4 million visitors in 2018, according to a survey by the World Tourism Organization (UNWTO). Ecotourism gained strength after the Covid-19 pandemic, with growing demand for destinations offering contact with nature and outdoor activities.

**Chapada dos Veadeiros** (Goiás) – Access to Almecegas Waterfall is via Pousada Fazenda São Bento. The park features trails, 80- and 120-meter waterfalls, and the Moon Valley (Vale da Lua), a set of rocks sculpted by the São Miguel River. The town of Alto Paraíso has the largest lodging offer in the region.

**Itatiaia National Park** (Rio de Janeiro), the country's first, is home to Agulhas Negras Peak and waterfalls such as Véu de Noiva and Itaporani. In Minas Gerais, **Ibitipoca State Park** has Monkeys Waterfall (Cachoeira dos Macacos) and Window to the Sky (Janela do Céu), a natural pool at the top of the trail. **Chapada Diamantina** (Bahia) offers three-day itineraries in Vale do Pati and access to Smoke Waterfall (Cachoeira da Fumaça).

**Fernando de Noronha** (Pernambuco) charges a R$ 179 fee for access to the marine national park, plus a R$ 92.89 state tax per day. The **Abrolhos archipelago** (Bahia) has entry at R$ 104 and hosts humpback whales from July to November. In **Foz do Iguaçu** (Paraná), entry to the Iguaçu Falls costs R$ 63.

Ecological tourism drives the economy of small municipalities surrounding conservation units. In **Bonito** (Mato Grosso do Sul), tours are sold exclusively by local agencies at fixed prices. **Jalapão State Park** (Tocantins) covers 158,000 hectares between Mateiros and São Félix do Tocantins.
