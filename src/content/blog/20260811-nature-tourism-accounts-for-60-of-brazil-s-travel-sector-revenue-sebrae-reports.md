Nature-based tourism in Brazil now accounts for approximately 60% of the sector's total revenue, according to data released in 2025 by Sebrae and the Ministry of Tourism. The figure reflects a broader market realignment, with more than 65% of travel companies now offering experiences tied to natural environments.

Ecotourism is consolidating its role as both an economic driver and a conservation tool, with 18 destinations identified in a recent survey. These areas combine biome protection with income generation for local communities, positioning the model as a credible alternative to mass tourism.

## Destinations leading the offer

- **Chapada dos Veadeiros (GO)** – A UNESCO World Natural Heritage Site featuring trails, waterfalls, and preserved Cerrado landscapes.
- **Lençóis Maranhenses (MA)** – A national park of seasonal lagoons set among dunes; the recommended visiting window runs from April to October.
- **Pantanal (MT)** – The world's largest continuous floodplain, renowned for wildlife observation including the jaguar.
- **Jalapão (TO)** – Natural springs, dunes, and mountain ranges in a sparsely populated stretch of the Cerrado.
- **Ilha de Marajó (PA)** – A fluvio-marine island of flooded grasslands, mangroves, and riverside traditions.

Sustainable travel operator Vivalá organizes expeditions to these destinations with an emphasis on community immersion. Itineraries include overnight stays in local homes, trails guided by indigenous community members, and cultural experiences, according to the company.

The Sebrae survey notes that most businesses operating in ecotourism regions are micro or small enterprises, underscoring the segment's role in distributing income more broadly. The Ministry of Tourism aims to raise the international profile of these destinations throughout 2025.
