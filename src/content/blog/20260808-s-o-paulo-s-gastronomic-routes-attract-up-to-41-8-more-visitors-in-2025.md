São Paulo state's Wine, Coffee, and Cheese Routes expanded their visitor numbers in 2025. The data comes from the Gastronomic Routes Performance and Profile Survey, a study by the Tourism Economy Intelligence Center (Ciet), linked to the São Paulo State Secretariat of Tourism and Travel (Setur-SP). The survey heard from 134 establishments participating in the three routes.

The cheese route recorded the largest flow growth, with an average increase of 41.8% over the previous period. The coffee route saw a 37.25% rise in visitation, while the wine route expanded by 15.9%. Revenue at the establishments also increased: 35.75% for coffee, 27.2% for cheese, and 12.4% for wine.

The average spending per person during peak months was R$199.29 on the coffee routes, R$185.50 on the wine routes, and R$123.94 on the cheese routes. The survey indicates that tourists from other cities in the state, from the state capital, from other states, and from abroad traveled the routes. On the coffee routes, for example, 24% of visitors came from other countries.

The Setur-SP survey also shows that joining the routes encouraged infrastructure investments. Among coffee producers, 96% of those who perceived a positive impact have carried out or are carrying out improvements, and 48% made new hires. On the cheese routes, 74% of establishments reported positive effects on their businesses.

## Other national routes

Outside São Paulo, structured itineraries also drive gastronomic tourism. In Minas Gerais, the Cheese Route covers the Serra da Canastra and the municipalities of Alagoa and Cruzília, with award-winning artisanal cheeses. In Rio Grande do Sul, the Wine Route includes the Vale dos Vinhedos and Garibaldi, known as the Brazilian capital of sparkling wine.

Gramado and Canela, in the Serra Gaúcha, run the Chocolate Route with factories such as Florybal, Caracol, and Prawer. In the North, Manaus and Belém feature dishes like tacacá, pato no tucupi, and pirarucu de casaca. Meanwhile, Salvador and Recife make up part of the Northeast's offerings with acarajé, moqueca, bolo de rolo, and tapioca, on itineraries that cross historic centers and public markets.
